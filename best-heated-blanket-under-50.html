What actually matters on a budget

  • Safety certification: look for a UL or ETL listing in the product details.
  • Auto shut-off: a timer that turns the blanket off is the single most useful safety feature, especially if you doze off on the couch.
  • Heat settings: more settings let you find a comfortable level; check the listing for the exact number.
  • Washability: most electric throws are machine washable once the controller is unplugged. Confirm in the care instructions.
  • Size: "throw" sizes suit one person on a couch; twin and larger cost more and often go above $50.

Prices move a lot from October through Cyber Monday, so a throw that's over $50 today may dip under it during a sale. Always check the live price.

Safety tips

Don't fold or bunch a heated blanket while it's on, don't use it with infants, and follow the manufacturer's instructions. People with diabetes or reduced sensation should check with a doctor first.
